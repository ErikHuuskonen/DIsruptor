# Predator Universal Survival Strategy Schema

## 1. Universal rule
**"Gain more energy than you spend catching it."** (optimal foraging)
A predator removes its prey and takes the full value at once. Every hunt is an investment that must return a profit.

## 2. Shared functions
Core problem: prey is built to escape (alarm sensors, escape engines, armor & weapons), and every hunt costs energy and risks injury.
Functions: detection, selection, capture, consumption.

## Master Loop
```
E → A → B → C → D → F
↑_______________________↑
  repeats when hungry, smarter each round
```

---

## E. TERRITORY & COMPETITION
```
a. claim hunting ground
b. exclude rivals
c. mark and patrol borders
d. monitor prey population (avoid over-hunting)
```

---

## A. DETECTION
```
a. sensing
   i.   visual motion detection
   ii.  scent trail reading
   iii. sound localization
   iv.  heat / vibration / electric field sensing

b. search
   i.   territory patrolling
   ii.  hotspot waiting (waterholes, migration routes)
   iii. trail tracking
   iv.  following signals of other species

c. concealment
   i.   camouflage
   ii.  downwind approach
   iii. silent movement
   iv.  timing to prey's low-alert periods

d. assessment
   i.   prey count estimation
   ii.  prey position mapping
   iii. escape route reading
   iv.  competitor presence check
```

---

## B. SELECTION
```
a. weakness detection
   i.   injury / limp reading
   ii.  age recognition (young, old)
   iii. sickness cues
   iv.  fatigue observation

b. isolation
   i.   straggler identification
   ii.  herd splitting
   iii. edge-of-group targeting
   iv.  separation from protectors

c. profit calculation
   i.   energy value estimation
   ii.  chase cost estimation
   iii. injury risk evaluation
   iv.  success probability estimation

d. commitment
   i.   go / no-go decision
   ii.  target lock
   iii. abort threshold setting
   iv.  switching to an easier target
```

---

## C. CAPTURE
```
a. approach
   i.   stalking
   ii.  closing distance unseen
   iii. ambush positioning
   iv.  lure / bait use

b. attack mode
   i.   ambush burst
   ii.  sustained pursuit
   iii. trap triggering
   iv.  coordinated team attack

c. escape blocking
   i.   route cutting
   ii.  cornering
   iii. driving into terrain or teammates
   iv.  grip and hold

d. strike
   i.   surprise timing
   ii.  weak-point targeting
   iii. venom / disabling
   iv.  speed burst
```

---

## D. CONSUMPTION
```
a. subduing
   i.   immobilization
   ii.  exhaustion
   iii. kill bite
   iv.  injury avoidance

b. securing
   i.   dragging to safety
   ii.  competitor intimidation
   iii. hiding the kill
   iv.  sharing by group hierarchy

c. extraction
   i.   most valuable parts first
   ii.  full-carcass use
   iii. fast intake
   iv.  leftover abandonment to scavengers

d. recovery
   i.   rest and digestion
   ii.  caching for later
   iii. energy reserve building
   iv.  return to territory
```

---

## F. LEARNING & ADAPTATION
```
a. learn from failed hunts
b. switch prey types when one becomes scarce
c. teach young hunting techniques
d. counter-adapt to prey defenses (arms race)
```

---

## 5. Gap analysis
Missing from A–D, added as E and F:
- **E. Territory & competition** — A–D assumed the predator has exclusive access to prey. Rivals and over-hunting were not covered.
- **F. Learning & adaptation** — prey evolves better defenses; a predator that does not learn falls behind.
